Science in plain language

Energy is the ability to cause change. Food, batteries, and sunlight are everyday forms of it. Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space. Atoms combine into molecules; molecules build materials, cells, and machines.

The scientific method: ask a question, form a hypothesis, test it, read the data, and revise the idea. Good science is reproducible: someone else should get similar results with the same steps.

  • Observe carefully and write what you see, not only what you expect.
  • Separate opinion from measurement.
  • Cite sources when you claim a fact.

History & civic literacy

Human societies keep records so later people can learn from success and failure. Primary sources (letters, laws, data) differ from secondary summaries. When you read the news, check who published it, when, and what evidence they show.

  • Ask: What is the claim? What is the evidence? What is missing?
  • Maps, timelines, and statistics need context—units, dates, and sample size.
  • Disagreement is normal; insults are not a substitute for arguments.

Digital literacy

Passwords protect accounts; long unique phrases beat short clever words. Phishing tries to look official so you hand over keys. Before you click a link, check the real domain. Before you install software, know the publisher.

  • Prefer official app stores and known publishers.
  • Do not reuse one password everywhere.
  • Public Wi‑Fi is convenient; sensitive tasks prefer trusted networks.

Study habits that work

Short focused blocks beat marathon cramming. Explain an idea out loud in your own words. Sleep and water are not optional extras—they are part of learning. When stuck, write the smallest next step, then do only that step.

  • Pomodoro-style: 25 minutes focus, 5 minutes break.
  • Active recall: close the notes and try to remember.
  • Spaced practice: review after 1 day, 1 week, 1 month.

Media & creativity

Design tools, classrooms, and creative apps are useful when you have a goal: a poster, a lesson, a prototype. Start with audience and message, then pick the tool. Constraints (time, size, colors) often improve the result.
